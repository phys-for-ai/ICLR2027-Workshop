Physical Pathways to Understanding AI Mechanism Workshop

Exploring how physical principles can shape learning objectives, illuminate optimization dynamics, and reveal the structure of AI representations.

Introduction

Modern AI systems are increasingly powerful, yet their objectives, representations, training dynamics, and emergent behaviors remain only partially understood. The Physical Pathways to Understanding AI Mechanism workshop explores how physical principles can provide mathematical and algorithmic tools for understanding and designing modern machine learning systems.

The workshop treats AI systems as high-dimensional statistical and dynamical systems. From this perspective, learning objectives define effective energy landscapes, optimization and sampling induce nonequilibrium dynamics, and latent spaces become structured state spaces shaped by geometry, symmetry, and constraints. This viewpoint connects a broad range of topics, including free-energy principles, diffusion and flow models, energy-based learning, representation geometry, phase transitions, training dynamics, and foundation-model theory.

Unlike broad AI-for-science workshops that focus primarily on applying AI to scientific discovery, this workshop studies AI systems themselves as objects of physical analysis. We aim to bring together researchers from machine learning, statistical physics, applied mathematics, computational neuroscience, and related fields to build a shared language for Physics for AI, clarify open problems, and identify principles for interpretable, controllable, and robust AI systems.

Call for Paper

Topics of Interest

We welcome submissions on topics related to Physical Pathways to Understanding AI Mechanism, including but not limited to:

  • Physical principles for AI objectives
    • Free-energy, variational, and thermodynamic principles for machine learning
    • Physical perspectives on generalization, robustness, and out-of-distribution behavior
    • Connections between physical principles and objectives in VAEs, diffusion models, flow models, etc.
  • Generative models as dynamical systems
    • Diffusion models, flow matching, energy-based models, and iterative refinement as dynamical processes
    • Nonequilibrium, thermodynamic, and stochastic-process views of generative modeling
    • Distributional dynamics, sampling dynamics, and trajectories in high-dimensional spaces
    • Controllability, steerability, interpolation, sampling efficiency, and generative geometry

Submission Format

  • The workshop is non-archival. Submissions should not be identical to finalized work that has already been published or accepted at major machine learning venues.
  • Submissions may take the form of original research, work in progress, theoretical notes, benchmark proposals, position papers, negative results, or empirical studies. We accept two submission formats:
    • Short papers: up to 4 pages, excluding references and optional appendices. This format is suitable for early-stage ideas, position papers, theoretical notes, negative results, and concise empirical or conceptual studies.
    • Long papers: up to 8 pages, excluding references and optional appendices. This format is suitable for more developed research contributions with substantial theoretical, empirical, or methodological content.
  • All submissions should follow the ICLR workshop style. Reviews will be double-blind, so submissions should be anonymized. Authors may include appendices or supplementary material, but reviewers are not required to read them.
  • All submissions must be made through OpenReview. Accepted submissions, whether short or long, will be presented as posters. A small number of accepted submissions will additionally be selected for 10-minute spotlight presentations.
